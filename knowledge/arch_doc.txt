ARCHITECTURE DECISION RECORD (ADR) STANDARDS

Standard ADR structure
1. Title — short, descriptive, present tense (e.g. "Use Azure Service Bus for cross-service messaging")
2. Status — Proposed / Accepted / Deprecated / Superseded
3. Context — the problem, constraints, and forces at play. No solution yet, just the situation.
4. Decision — the chosen approach, stated clearly and in the affirmative ("We will...")
5. Consequences — both positive and negative outcomes of the decision, including tradeoffs accepted.
6. Alternatives considered — other options evaluated and why they were rejected.

Writing conventions
- Use active voice and present/future tense for the Decision section.
- Avoid vague qualifiers like "should probably" or "might want to" — ADRs record firm decisions, not suggestions.
- Keep each ADR focused on a single decision; split unrelated decisions into separate documents.
- Date and author/owner should be included in the header for traceability.

Stakeholder-ready summary conventions
- Lead with business impact (cost, risk, timeline) before technical detail.
- Avoid unexplained acronyms; define on first use.
- Keep to one page where possible; link out to the full technical design for detail.

Technical design document conventions
- Include: Overview, Goals/Non-Goals, Proposed Architecture (with diagram description), Data Flow, Failure Modes, Rollout Plan, Open Questions.
- Non-Goals are as important as Goals — explicitly state what is out of scope to prevent scope creep during review.
- Failure Modes section should cover at least: what happens on partial failure, retry behavior, and data consistency guarantees.
